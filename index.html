A Brief History of English

From a Germanic dialect on a foggy island to a language of a billion voices, English has been rewritten by every conquest, printing press and revolution.

c. 450–1150

Old English

Anglo-Saxon settlers bring the tongue that gives us Beowulf and our oldest everyday words.

1150–1500

Middle English

After the Norman Conquest, French floods in. Chaucer writes The Canterbury Tales in the language of the people.

1500–1700

The Renaissance

Printing standardises spelling. Shakespeare coins hundreds of words and the King James Bible shapes the cadence of prose.

1700–1900

Enlightenment to Gothic Romance

Johnson compiles his Dictionary; Austen, the Brontës, Shelley and Stoker give fiction its shadows.

1835–1857

English arrives in India

Macaulay's Minute (1835) and Wood's Despatch (1854) lead to the universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras in 1857.

1900–today

Many Englishes

Tagore, R.K. Narayan, Rushdie, Roy and Ghosh make Indian writing in English a world literature of its own.
